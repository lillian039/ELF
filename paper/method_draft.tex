Attribute Superposition in Low-Rank Conditioning of Language Flows}

\begin{abstract}
Continuous, embedding-space language models such as ELF generate text by denoising
a sequence of frozen encoder embeddings. We ask what happens when their
conditioning is routed through an explicit, low-rank \emph{semantic code}: factor
the embedding as $\xb=\phib(s)+\rb$ and flow the residual conditioned on a
learned code $\phib(s)=U\cb$, $\cb\in\R^{k}$. The motivating intuition is that a
compact bottleneck yields cleaner, more disentangled control. We find the
opposite. A low-rank code is no more controllable than full-rank mean-pool
conditioning, and on a decontaminated test it is \emph{more} entangled: probed
superposition rises strictly monotonically as $k$ shrinks and tracks off-target
leakage across code sizes ($r{=}0.90$). Within a model the probe does not
predict which pairs leak; the target does: a preregistered out-of-sample test
shows leakage flows into attributes the denoiser already varies at fixed
conditioning, weighted by single-token movability (cell-level $r{=}0.87$ on
held-out attributes). Steering does not inject off-target content; it biases
variation the denoiser already produces. Interventions localize the mechanism:
a decorrelation regularizer games its own metric, diverges, or destroys
steerability; forcing the data correlation changes nothing; projecting the
conditioning vector off every off-target direction at inference changes
nothing in seven models. The vector is a pointer, and the leak is
manufactured in the denoiser; a counterfactual penalty on that pathway helps
only where aimed, and the pressure resurfaces on untrained routes. A
preregistered replication on professional biographies compounds the result:
there the bottleneck also degrades on-target control while leaking more per
unit of it, and one model severs its denoiser's code read entirely yet leaks
worst in the fleet through the $\phi$-conditioned decoder head, invisible to
every code-space diagnostic. A depth-window instrument places the code's
entry in the first layers of every model: control and leakage separate along
trajectory time, not depth. We caution against compact semantic bottlenecks
as a disentanglement device.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ===========================================================================

ELF~\cite{elf2026} models text as a continuous flow in the token-embedding space of
a frozen encoder $E$: a full-attention Transformer denoises an entire embedding
sequence $\xb\in\R^{L\times d}$ from Gaussian noise to clean embeddings,
discretizing to tokens only at $t{=}1$ via a shared decoder head. Global sentence
structure is \emph{already} present, since every position attends to every other at
every step, but only implicitly, distributed across attention and never exposed as a
manipulable object.

A natural idea, and the one we study, is to factor that structure into an explicit,
sentence-level \emph{semantic code} $\phib(s)$ and flow only the residual
$\rb=\xb-\phib(s)$ conditioned on it. Making $\phib(s)=U\cb$ a learned low-rank code
$\cb\in\R^{k}$ turns the conditioning variable's dimensionality and geometry into
knobs we can set and probe. The motivating intuition, shared with much of the
disentanglement literature, is that squeezing conditioning through a compact
``semantic manifold'' should concentrate reusable global structure and yield cleaner,
more separable control.

We test this intuition on TinyStories, then preregister a replication on Bias
in Bios biographies, and it holds on neither. The low-rank code is no
more controllable than full-rank mean-pool conditioning (on the harder corpus it
is measurably \emph{less}), and once we decontaminate
the steering axis of correlated attributes it disentangles \emph{worse}. Rather than
stop at a negative result, we ask \emph{why}, and find a concrete mechanism:
squeezing many attributes through a low-rank code forces them into
\emph{superposition}, which we measure directly with the Manifold Probe of
\cite{manifold2026} and localize, by intervention, in the bottleneck's capacity and
the generation pathway rather than in the data or the readout axes.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i) A controlled study of conditioning geometry in an embedding-space language flow,
varying the code dimension $k$ from $8$ to $512$ under matched budget
(\S\ref{sec:method}--\ref{sec:exp}).
(ii) The negative finding: a low-rank semantic code does not improve controllability
or disentanglement over full-rank conditioning, and is measurably more entangled on
a decontaminated test (\S\ref{sec:leak}).
(iii) The mechanism, measured directly: probed superposition grows strictly
monotonically as $k$ shrinks and tracks leakage across code sizes ($r{=}0.90$), but
within a model it does not rank which pairs leak; the target does, and a
preregistered out-of-sample test isolates the target property: leakage flows into
attributes the denoiser varies naturally at fixed code, weighted by single-token
movability (cell-level $r{=}0.87$ on four held-out attributes;
\S\ref{sec:superpos}--\ref{sec:law}).
(iv) Evidence the entanglement is \emph{intrinsic} and lives in the generation
pathway: a decorrelation regularizer fails by metric gaming, divergence, or
steerability collapse, and inference-time projection of the conditioning vector off
all off-target directions changes nothing in seven models (\S\ref{sec:mitig}).
(v) A causal null on the data side: forcing the sentiment--gender correlation across
$[-0.3,+0.3]$ at fixed marginals leaves superposition and leakage unchanged at
$k{=}64$ and $k{=}16$ (\S\ref{sec:rho}).
(vi) A preregistered second-corpus replication (Bias in Bios biographies) in
which the capacity harm transfers and compounds: the low-rank code loses
on-target control while leaking more per unit of it, and a two-round
preregistered test shows the targeting law replicates on source-independent
targets for every functional-code model ($\rho\,0.31\!\to\!0.60$ once the
test set's source confound is removed by a frozen screening rule;
\S\ref{sec:bib}).
(vii) A depth-window instrument (parallel $\phi$ token groups, per-layer key
masks) that closes the localization grid: in every model the code is read in
the first quartile of layers, control and leakage share that entry point,
and the pressure differentiates along trajectory time; the same instrument
anatomizes the one law-violating model, whose denoiser is bitwise
$\phi$-invariant while its leak rides the $\phi$-conditioned decoder head
(\S\ref{sec:depth}).
(viii) A minimal model (bottleneck, generator, manifold snap) that reproduces all
four regularities, giving the chain capacity $\to$ overlap $\to$ biased natural
variation $\to$ leakage a mechanistic reading (\S\ref{sec:toy}).

\section{Related work}
% ===========================================================================

\paragraph{Continuous / embedding-space language models.} Diffusion and flow language
models generate in a continuous latent rather than autoregressively over tokens:
Diffusion-LM~\cite{diffusionlm} denoises word-embedding sequences, and
ELF~\cite{elf2026} flows in a frozen encoder's embedding space with a shared decoder
head. We take ELF as our backbone and study how an added conditioning variable
behaves, not the generative quality of the backbone itself.

\paragraph{Controllable generation.} Steering generation toward attributes has been
approached by plug-and-play gradients on a classifier~\cite{pplm} and, more recently,
by moving along linear directions in a model's representation space. Our steering
protocol (fit an attribute axis, move along it) is standard; our focus is the
\emph{geometry} of the conditioning code that such steering acts on.

\paragraph{Disentanglement.} A large line of work seeks factorized latents, e.g.\
$\beta$-VAE~\cite{betavae}. Locatello et al.~\cite{locatello} showed that
\emph{unsupervised} disentanglement is unidentifiable without inductive biases or
supervision. We differ in setting and claim: we work in \emph{supervised,
conditional generation}, and rather than seek identifiability we identify and
directly measure a specific geometric failure mode: superposition induced by a
low-rank conditioning bottleneck under correlated attributes.

\paragraph{Superposition and mechanistic interpretability.} Neural representations
can place more features than dimensions by encoding them as non-orthogonal
directions~\cite{elhage}. The Manifold Probe~\cite{manifold2026} generalizes linear
probing to recover the feature subspace and encoding directions of a concept. We
apply it to a generative model's conditioning code and relate the recovered
superposition to a behavioral quantity (off-target leakage).

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
% ===========================================================================

\paragraph{Semantic / residual factorization.} Let $\xb=E(s)\in\R^{L\times d}$ be the
frozen, normalized encoder embeddings of a sentence $s$ and $\bm m\in\{0,1\}^{L}$ mask
the valid positions. We split the embedding additively into a sentence-level code
$\phib(s)$ (broadcast over $L$) and a residual, and, unlike ELF, which models
$p(\xb)$, model the conditional residual $p(\rb\mid\phib(s))$:
\begin{equation}
  \xb=\phib(s)+\rb,\qquad \rb=\xb-\phib(s).
  \label{eq:split}
\end{equation}
The full-rank baseline (M1) sets $\phib(s)$ to the masked mean of the clean latent,
$\phib(s)=\big(\sum_i m_i\xb_i\big)/\sum_i m_i\in\R^{d}$.

\paragraph{Conditional flow.} Keeping ELF's flow-matching convention with noise scale
$\sigma$, $\zb_t=t\,\rb+(1-t)\sigma\eps$, $\eps\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$, we flow the
\emph{residual}: the network predicts $\hat\rb$, giving velocity
$\hat{\bm v}=(\hat\rb-\zb_t)/(1-t)$, and $\phib(s)$ is injected as $P{=}4$ learnable
conditioning prefix tokens ($\phib{=}\bm 0$ gives the classifier-free-guidance
branch). The denoiser loss is masked velocity regression,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{fm}}=\E_{s,t,\eps}\Big[\bm m\odot
  \big\|\bm v_\theta(\zb_t,\phib(s),t)-(\rb-\zb_t)/(1-t)\big\|^2\Big].
  \label{eq:fm}
\end{equation}
Because tokenization needs the full embedding, ELF's shared decoder head operates on
$\xb$ (reconstructed as $\hat\xb=\phib+\hat\rb$ at $t{=}1$) under the usual
cross-entropy $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ce}}$; the baseline objective is
$\mathcal L_{\text{M1}}=(1-p_{\text{dec}})\mathcal L_{\mathrm{fm}}
+p_{\text{dec}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ce}}$.

\paragraph{Low-rank code (M2).} The object of study replaces the mean with a learned
low-rank code, $\cb=g_\theta(\mathrm{pool}(\xb))\in\R^{k}$, lifted by a bias-free
$U\in\R^{d\times k}$ to $\phib(s)=U\cb$. Here $g_\theta$ is an MLP
($\mathrm{GELU}$ hidden layer $d\!\to\!256$, then linear heads for
$\bm\mu,\log\bm\sigma^2\in\R^{k}$); the forward pass uses $\cb=\bm\mu$. Setting
$k{=}d$, $U{=}I$, $g_\theta{=}\mathrm{pool}$ recovers M1. Two auxiliary terms shape
the code: a semantic-consistency (cycle) loss that re-probes the reconstruction and
matches its code to the conditioning code,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{cyc}}=\E\big[\|g_\theta(\mathrm{pool}(\hat\xb))-\mathrm{sg}[\cb]\|^2\big],
  \label{eq:cyc}
\end{equation}
so the trained model is optimized to realize a perturbed conditioning code; and an
information bottleneck $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ib}}=\beta\,\mathrm{KL}(q(\cb\mid s)\,\|\,
\mathcal N(0,I))$ that guards against collapse. The residual target uses a
stop-gradient lift $\rb=\xb-\mathrm{sg}[U\cb]$, so $g_\theta,U$ are trained through
the conditioning, cycle, and IB paths. The full objective is
$\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\text{M1}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{cyc}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{cyc}}
+\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ib}}$.

\paragraph{Steering and leakage.} Given a labeled attribute we fit an axis $\bm u$ in
code space (difference of class means), then generate from $\cb_0+\alpha\bm u$ across a
sweep of $\alpha$, lifting $\phib=U(\cb_0+\alpha\bm u)$. Because attributes are
correlated in the data, a raw axis is contaminated; for the disentanglement test we
decontaminate the sentiment axis by projecting out the gender direction,
$\bm u_\perp=\bm u_{\text{sent}}-(\bm u_{\text{sent}}^{\!\top}\hat{\bm u}_{\text{gen}})
\hat{\bm u}_{\text{gen}}$, and steer $\bm u_\perp$. On-target \emph{control} is the
change in the target-attribute fraction across $\alpha$; off-target \emph{leakage} is
the change in a held-out attribute that should stay fixed.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Setup}
We use a 50k-story subset of TinyStories~\cite{tinystories} with a frozen T5-small
encoder ($d{=}512$) and the ELF-B backbone ($\approx$105M parameters). Every model is
trained for 60 epochs at effective global batch 80 with AdamW under identical
hyperparameters apart from the code, on RTX-3090 GPUs. Each code size $k$ is an
\emph{independent} model (the lift $U$ and code MLP change shape with $k$, so weights
cannot be shared); M1 is the full-rank mean-pool reference. Sentiment and
protagonist-gender labels come from small dependency-free lexicons applied to the
original stories. For leakage we report two metrics: a saturating \emph{fraction
range} (max$-$min of a binary attribute fraction over $\alpha$), and a non-saturating
\emph{logit-shift} from a held-out linear gender classifier (difference-of-means in
the frozen embedding space, trained on real stories and applied to generations),
plus an AUC between the steering extremes. Unless noted, leakage numbers are averaged
over five evaluation seeds (bootstrapped axis/classifier and resampled generation
noise).

\subsection{The code is used, and control is at parity}
The factorization is healthy: at a fixed code, resampling noise yields semantically
consistent yet lexically diverse stories, and a generation's re-probed code matches
its conditioning code (Table~\ref{tab:c1}). Steering the sentiment axis flips the
held-out positive-sentiment fraction monotonically from $0$ to $1$ for \emph{both} the
low-rank and full-rank models, with coherent generations at the extremes. On these
controllability metrics SM-ELF and full-rank Conditional-ELF are indistinguishable:
the low-rank manifold and cycle loss confer no measurable advantage for single-factor
control.

\subsection{The low-rank code harms disentanglement}
\label{sec:leak}
Sentiment and gender are correlated in the data (positive stories skew female
protagonist), so a raw sentiment axis drags gender entirely for every model. The
models separate only once we steer the \emph{decontaminated} axis (gender projected
out): the model's own code geometry then sets how much gender still leaks. A
single-seed behavioral read already shows the low-rank code leaking more, but that
statistic saturates at its $[0,1]$ ceiling and is run-to-run noisy, so we rely on the
five-seed, non-saturating measurement (Table~\ref{tab:leakrobust}).

The robust picture is nuanced but consistent. The mean gender logit-shift
\emph{decreases} as the code grows across the learned bottleneck range
$k{=}8\!\to\!16\!\to\!64\!\to\!256$ ($1.31\!>\!0.97\!>\!0.76\!>\!0.45$), and the
endpoint is cleanly separated from the mean-pool floor ($k{=}16$: $0.97\!\pm\!0.15$
vs.\ M1: $0.37\!\pm\!0.06$); adjacent $k$ overlap at five evaluation seeds, so we
claim the trend and the endpoint gap rather than strict step-by-step separation.
Additional model seeds support this reading and sharpen it
(Table~\ref{tab:seeds}). The probe's aggregate readings replicate almost
digit-for-digit and the behavioral endpoint story holds, but adjacent-$k$
point ordering is not stable across model seeds, and the capacity collapse we
placed at $k{=}8$ has a seed-dependent boundary: two of three $k{=}16$ seeds
lose most of their on-target control while their probed capacity barely
moves. Aggregate capacity pressure is a highly reproducible function of $k$;
the per-seed behavioral consequences at a fixed small $k$ are not.

Two departures from a pure
dimensionality story are informative. \emph{Capacity collapse at $k{=}8$:} on-target
control, $1.0$ at every larger $k$, drops to $0.63\!\pm\!0.06$: the extreme bottleneck
can no longer represent the steered attribute, so leakage and control fail together.
\emph{Parametrization offset at $k{=}d$:} the learned $k{=}512$ code leaks
$0.81\!\pm\!0.29$, far above the mean-pool M1's $0.37$ at equal dimension, even though
its measured superposition is correctly low (\S\ref{sec:superpos}): the learned
conditioning stack introduces some entanglement of its own, beyond the dimensional
effect.

\subsection{Directly measuring superposition}
\label{sec:superpos}
The behavioral sweep is consistent with superposition, but does not by itself
distinguish it from a generic loss of capacity. We test the mechanism directly with
the Manifold Probe~\cite{manifold2026}, applied to the code space. For each attribute
$a\in\{\text{sentiment, gender, animal, length}\}$ with scalar label $z$, the probe
learns the smooth features $f(z)=\bm\beta^\top h(z)$ linearly predictable from the
codes $\cb$ (a generalized-eigenproblem closed form) together with their encoding
directions, recovering the attribute's encoding \emph{subspace}
$\mathcal Q_a\subseteq\R^{k}$ (its \emph{manifold dimension} is the rank of that
subspace). We quantify superposition geometrically: the pairwise interference
$\mathrm{overlap}(\mathcal Q_a,\mathcal Q_b)$ (mean squared cosine of principal
angles, in $[0,1]$), its mean over off-diagonal pairs, and a \emph{capacity ratio}
$\sum_a\dim\mathcal Q_a/k$. We use readout (linear-probe) directions, the geometry
governing linear separability, and average over five splits on $N{=}1500$ held-out
stories. On synthetic data these read $\approx 0$ for attributes on orthogonal axes
and large for attributes packed into a shared direction, and we subtract the
random-direction baseline $1/k$, so a positive value is not an artifact of small $k$.

Every superposition measure increases strictly monotonically as $k$ shrinks, across
all six code sizes and five seeds; this is the cleanest trend in the study
(Table~\ref{tab:superpos}). The interference of the steering pair itself
(sentiment vs.\ gender, the attributes that leak) rises monotonically from $0.003$ at
the mean-pool reference to $0.138$ at $k{=}8$, and tracks the robust leakage
logit-shift at Pearson $r{=}0.90$ over all six points (rising to $r{=}0.97$, Spearman
$\rho{=}1.0$, once the learned $k{=}512$ outlier is set aside). That outlier is itself
diagnostic: its behavioral leakage is inflated by the parametrization offset while its
measured superposition is correctly low, the dissociation one expects when
entanglement comes from the conditioning stack rather than the bottleneck. Where the
dimensional mechanism applies, shrinking the code forces attributes to share encoding
directions, and the amount of sharing co-varies with how strongly steering one
attribute drags the others. The packing is only loosely geometric even in code space:
across all 36 (model, pair) cells, pairwise interference correlates with the demand
ratio $(\dim\mathcal Q_a+\dim\mathcal Q_b)/k$ at $r{=}0.62$ ($R^2{=}0.38$), so
measured subspace dimensions predict the broad scale of interference but not its
pair-level detail.

\paragraph{The correlation is between models, not between pairs.} The $r{=}0.90$
above compares \emph{models} (one point per $k$). A finer-grained mechanism, in which
pairs that share more subspace leak more into each other, would predict the same relation
\emph{within} a model. It does not hold. Measuring all twelve directed pairs among
our four attributes per model (each source axis decontaminated against all other
attributes, one generation batch scored against every off-target classifier), the
within-model rank correlation between pairwise interference and pairwise leakage is
\emph{negative} on average (Spearman $-0.53$; positive in one of six models), and
pooled regressions with model fixed effects assign interference no incremental
explanatory power ($R^2$ $0.580\!\to\!0.592$; directed version $F(1,62){=}0.55$).
Held out, an interference-based predictor is \emph{worse} than predicting each
model's mean leakage for every pair (RMSE $0.31$ vs.\ $0.26$).

What \emph{does} structure the directed pairs is the identity of the target
attribute: over all 72 (model, source, target) cells, model fixed effects explain
$R^2{=}0.24$ of directed leakage, adding target effects raises this to $0.52$,
source effects add $0.01$, and interference adds $0.001$. Leakage flows into gender
($+0.19$ in calibrated logit units) and animal ($+0.21$) and away from sentiment
($-0.26$) and length ($-0.14$), consistently across models and sources, and the
split is not explained by base rate or source steerability. The probe, then, reads
\emph{how much} capacity pressure a code is under; \emph{which} pairs pay for it is
a property of the target, which \S\ref{sec:law} isolates and makes predictive.

\subsection{The leak follows the denoiser's own variation, predictively}
\label{sec:law}
Target identity is not yet a mechanism. To find the property behind it we scored
candidate predictors by \emph{preregistered out-of-sample prediction}: new
lexicon attributes are added to the protocol as scoring targets only (the same
generations are scored against more held-out classifiers, so the behavioral data
cannot leak into the choice of predictor), predictions for the new targets are
written down, then their leakage is measured. Target structure itself replicates
on held-out attributes: over twelve targets and seven models (308 directed
cells), target effects still add $R^2$ $0.68\!\to\!0.74$ over model effects.

Candidate predictors and their fates are summarized in
Table~\ref{tab:predictors}. Every data-side candidate fails; evidence
\emph{redundancy} even anti-predicts (gender, the most redundant attribute,
ranks least fragile, opposite to its observed absorbency). What succeeds is a
\emph{model-side} quantity. Since the interventions of \S\ref{sec:mitig}
place the leak inside the denoiser, we measured each attribute's
\emph{natural variation}: at a fixed conditioning code, resampling only the
generation noise, the standard deviation of the attribute's held-out
classifier logit across samples. Natural variation correlates positively with
absorbency inside every model (Spearman $+0.29$ to $+0.69$), and a second
preregistration held its three-parameter combination with insertion
movability to account: fit on the eight measured attributes, predictions
registered for four fresh ones (color, plant, water, question) over all 28
(model, target) cells, then measured. The predictions hit (ordering Spearman
$0.80$, a single adjacent swap; cell-level Pearson $0.87$), and the relation
survives every training condition in the study: pooling the $\phi$-retrained
models of \S\ref{sec:rho} (both seeds), the extra $k$-sweep seeds, and the
$\lambda{=}1$ decorrelation model, twenty models and over $850$ directed
cells in all, the natvar--leakage correlation is positive in every single
model.

The picture is
mechanistic and simple: steering does not force new content into the
generation, it \emph{biases variation the denoiser already produces}. Dialogue
is the clean dissociation: highly movable by insertion, but the denoiser never
varies it at fixed code (flip probability $0.000$ at $k{=}64$), and it resists
leakage; plant and water, which the denoiser varies freely, absorb at the level
of gender. The bottleneck sets how much pressure exists
(\S\ref{sec:superpos}); the denoiser's variation modes set where it lands.

\subsection{A natural fix fails, revealing the entanglement is intrinsic}
\label{sec:mitig}
If leakage were merely a matter of non-orthogonal readout axes, penalizing that
non-orthogonality during training should remove it. We add a decorrelation term
$\lambda_{\mathrm{dec}}\cos^2(\bm u_{\text{sent}},\bm u_{\text{gen}})$ on the
code-space difference-of-means axes (formed over the global batch) and sweep
$\lambda_{\mathrm{dec}}\in\{1,3,10\}$ at $k{=}64$ under matched budget, evaluating
every run at its last readable checkpoint with both the Manifold Probe and the
directed-pair leakage protocol of \S\ref{sec:superpos}. The regularizer fails in
three distinct ways as $\lambda$ grows (Table~\ref{tab:mitig}).

\emph{Metric gaming at $\lambda{=}1$.} The mildest penalty optimizes exactly what it
measures and nothing else: the trained cosine falls ($\cos^2\approx0.13\!\to\!0.05$)
while probed sentiment--gender interference \emph{triples} and leakage rises across
pairs (Table~\ref{tab:mitig}). Squeezing the one measured axis pair pushes the
attributes into \emph{other} shared directions that the probe sees and the trained
metric does not. Notably, probe interference and leakage move \emph{together} here:
the aggregate-thermometer reading of \S\ref{sec:superpos} survives an intervention
even though pair-level ranking does not.

\emph{Destabilization.} All settings tend to diverge (velocity loss blowing up by
three to six orders of magnitude): $\lambda{=}1$ at $\sim$ep~57, $\lambda{=}3$ at
ep~39--45 depending on seed, $\lambda{=}10$ at $\sim$ep~45.

\emph{Steerability collapse at high $\lambda$.} The two runs reaching high effective
penalty (the surviving $\lambda{=}3$ seed and $\lambda{=}10$) expose the third
failure mode. $\lambda{=}10$ even achieves the nominal goal, pushing probed
interference below baseline, but steering either model does nothing: control $0.00$,
AUC $0.5$ on all twelve pairs, while the probe still recovers attribute subspaces of
normal capacity from their codes. The code still \emph{encodes} the attributes; the
denoiser has learned to ignore it. (We cannot fully exclude degraded generation
quality at these checkpoints; either way the models are useless for control.)

Across the sweep there is no setting that reduces leakage: the regularizer either
reroutes the entanglement ($\lambda{=}1$), destroys training, or disconnects the
code from generation ($\lambda{\geq}3$). The entanglement is intrinsic to
conditioning on a low-rank code rather than a removable artifact of the readout
axes.

\paragraph{Inference-time surgery on the conditioning vector also fails, completing
the localization.} If the leaked content rode into generation on the conditioning
vector, it could be removed at inference: steer in code space as usual, but project
the injected delta $\Delta\phib=U(\alpha\bm u_\perp)$ off the span of the off-target
attributes' embedding classifier directions before conditioning the denoiser. Run
under the full directed-pair protocol on all six code sizes and the $\lambda{=}1$
model, this changes nothing: leakage moves by at most $8\%$ on the targeted pair and
$3\%$ on the twelve-pair mean (Table~\ref{tab:embproj}), against a protocol rerun
noise of $0.004$, so these are precise nulls. The reason is diagnostic: the injected
delta is already nearly orthogonal to the off-target directions (the projection
removes $3.7\%$ of its norm at $k{=}64$). There is no linearly readable off-target
content in the conditioning signal to remove; the drift is manufactured
\emph{downstream}, by the denoiser's learned response to the on-target component.
A sparse-dictionary decomposition shows this null is not an artifact of the
linear basis, and sharpens it: with a 4096-feature sparse autoencoder trained
on pooled story embeddings (instrument validated on the embedding-space
sentiment direction, which activates sentiment-associated features at
$4.5\times$ the random-feature baseline), the injected delta's feature
attribution is at chance for \emph{every} attribute, including sentiment
itself. The conditioning vector is a pointer the denoiser interprets, not a
payload carrying content; there is nothing in it to excise, for the off-target
attributes or even for the steered one.

The interventions thus bracket the leak: it survives decontamination of the code
axis, decorrelation during training, decorrelation of the data (\S\ref{sec:rho}),
and decontamination of the injected vector. What remains is the denoiser's
conditional response, which the next paragraph attacks directly.

\paragraph{A counterfactual penalty on the generation pathway itself.}
The localization prescribes where a remedy must act. We therefore add, at every
denoiser step, a second forward pass with the code steered along the per-batch
sentiment axis (gender projected out), $\cb'=\cb+\alpha\hat{\bm u}_\perp$ with
$\alpha\sim\pm U[1,3]$, and penalize the movement of the counterfactual
reconstruction's pooled embedding along the frozen gender direction,
$\lambda_{\mathrm{cf}}\,\big((\mathrm{pool}(\hat\xb')-\mathrm{pool}(\hat\xb))^{\!\top}
\hat{\bm w}_g/\sigma_g\big)^2$, the factual side stop-gradiented: the denoiser may
move sentiment but must not move gender while doing so. Getting this to train at
all required two structural precautions, each exposing a further defense of the
entanglement. With gradients allowed into the code stack, the model inflates the
code norm until fixed-magnitude steers are relatively negligible (the IB term
grows by three orders of magnitude before the velocity loss diverges): the same
metric-gaming family as \S\ref{sec:mitig}, now against an output-space penalty.
With the steer scaled by the live code RMS instead, the cycle loss chases the
counterfactually reshaped denoiser and the two spiral into a feedback runaway.
The stable form restricts the gradient to the denoiser (every manifold-side
quantity stop-gradiented), uses constant-scale steers (the IB anchors the code
RMS), and applies no counterfactual pressure for the first 12 epochs.
Table~\ref{tab:cf} summarizes the weight sweep; each row is a diagnostic the
mechanism account makes meaningful. At $\lambda_{\mathrm{cf}}{=}1$ the penalty
is satisfied exactly at the level it is imposed, and defeated one level up:
the terminal-step drift it targets is almost eliminated and probed
interference is untouched, but text-level on-target control collapses, every
source's steerability is muted (mean directed leakage falls only in
proportion), the protected pair does not improve, and the generation
distribution drifts (positive-sentiment rate $0.99$). The mechanism account
of \S\ref{sec:law} makes a sharper demand than ``leakage goes down'': an
effective generation-pathway fix should reduce the denoiser's \emph{natural
variation} of the protected attribute together with its leakage. The opposite
happens, natvar(gender) doubles, diagnosing the run as a distributional
retreat, not a disentanglement. At the other end,
$\lambda_{\mathrm{cf}}{=}0.1$ vanishes without trading anything: control, the
protected pair, natvar, and interference all sit at baseline; a run that did
not change the denoiser's generative habit, and correspondingly did not
change its leakage.

The middle of the curve is where the penalty finally works as designed, and
its success is as diagnostic as the failures. At $\lambda_{\mathrm{cf}}{=}0.3$
the protected pair improves $35\%$ at small control cost, the terminal-step
leak realization is eliminated, and, exactly as the mechanism account
demands, the denoiser's \emph{natural} gender flipping at fixed code is wiped
out: suppressing the variation mode suppresses the leakage that rides it,
closing the law-to-intervention causal loop. But the protection is
\emph{route-specific}: leakage into gender from a source the counterfactual
never trained on doubles, and the twelve-pair mean does not improve
(Table~\ref{tab:cf}). A single-direction penalty redistributes the
bottleneck's pressure rather than removing it. The first intervention in this
study that moves leakage in the right direction therefore also confirms the
thesis: the entanglement behaves as a conserved pressure that yields only
where it is individually pinned, and a complete fix would need counterfactual
coverage of the full source--target set, which we leave to future work.

\subsection{Data correlation is not the driver}
\label{sec:rho}
The account so far has treated attribute correlation in the data as a precondition:
correlated attributes, forced into a small code, come to share directions. TinyStories
gives this assumption little to work with (the lexicon-labeled sentiment--gender
association in our corpus is weak, $\phi\approx0.03$--$0.10$), so we test it
causally. We re-generate the 50k-story training set by quota sampling from the full
corpus, holding each attribute's marginal frequency at its natural value while
setting the sentiment--gender association to
$\phi\in\{-0.30,\,0,\,+0.15,\,+0.30\}$, and retrain the $k{=}64$ model on each
(matched budget, identical hyperparameters); the $\phi{=}0$ pipeline control isolates
the resampling itself. To test for an interaction with capacity pressure we repeat
the endpoints at $k{=}16$.

Neither measured superposition nor leakage responds to the manipulation
(Table~\ref{tab:rho}; two model seeds per $k{=}64$ cell). Both quantities are
non-monotone in $\phi$, with interference lowest at $\phi{=}+0.15$ (below the
$\phi{=}0$ control) and leakage highest at $\phi{=}-0.30$, directionally opposite to
the hypothesis; at $k{=}16$ the $\phi{=}+0.30$ model sits at or below its control on
both measures. Within-condition seed spread is as large as any between-condition
difference, and the one cell high in both seeds ($\phi{=}+0.30$ interference) is
ruled out as a dose-response by the tight, low $\phi{=}+0.15$ point. Meanwhile the
capacity effect reproduces inside this controlled pipeline: at $\phi{=}0$, shrinking
$k$ from 64 to 16 raises sentiment$\to$gender leakage from $0.52$ to $0.86$. A
sixty-fold change in data correlation does less to the code's entanglement than a
four-fold change in its dimension.

This reframes the mechanism. Superposition in the conditioning code is not the model
mirroring co-occurrence statistics; it is a property of how the training objective
packs attributes into limited dimensions, present even when the attributes are
statistically independent in the data. What moves it, in both directions and
monotonically, is $k$.

\subsection{On a harder corpus the harm replicates, and compounds}
\label{sec:bib}

professional biographies (Bias in Bios, first-sentence-removed
\texttt{hard\_text}): 50k bios under the identical architecture,
hyperparameters, and budget, steering an occupation macro-domain axis
(health-care vs.\ creative professions, 28\% and 22\% of the subset) with
gender again the off-target readout. Gates, endpoints, analysis rules, and the
one instrument amendment (a logistic-regression readout in the frozen space,
adopted when difference-of-means saturated at AUC $0.886$ on bios; the
logistic readout reaches $0.999$) were written down before the corresponding
measurements ran. The sanity gate passed at half training: fidelity $0.916$,
distinct-2 $0.895$, monotonic $0\!\to\!1$ on-target control for the full-rank
reference.

The capacity effect replicates, but not in the TinyStories form, because a
precondition of that form breaks: on this corpus the low-rank code degrades
on-target control itself (Table~\ref{tab:bib}, top row), from saturation at
full rank to $0.69/0.35$ across seeds at $k{=}16$, with no regularizer
involved. Raw logit-shifts are therefore confounded across models, exactly the
case the preregistered control-parity guard anticipated, and its fallback
metric applies: leakage per unit on-target shift. On that metric the low-rank
code is worse in both seeds at $k{=}16$, and both quantities are monotone in
$k$ at seed mean (normalized leakage $2.22 > 1.46 > 0.96 \approx 1.11$;
control $0.52 < 0.77 < 0.98 \approx 1.00$). Two corpus differences are
findings, not noise. First, the full-rank floor is roughly $3\times$ its
TinyStories value ($1.07$--$1.15$ vs.\ $0.37$): biographies are a
higher-baseline-entanglement corpus, and capacity pressure separates models
only at small $k$. Second, the control loss mirrors the steerability collapse
that decorrelation pressure induced on TinyStories (\S\ref{sec:mitig}), except
that here nothing induces it but the bottleneck. On the harder corpus a
compact code is worse on both sides of the ledger: less control, and more
leakage per unit of the control that remains. A third $k{=}16$ seed,
trained afterward to arbitrate how typical the severed-code outcome is,
answers both questions at once: its code is functional (probe $.227$
next to seed 1's $.232$, healthy natural variation, quality gate passed
at $+.06$), so the denoiser abandoning its code is a one-in-three
outcome rather than the mode, and its harm is the strongest of the
three: control $0.27$ with gender shift $1.83$, normalized leakage
$6.9$ against $1.7$ and $2.8$ for the first two seeds.

The probe sharpens the localization claim rather than merely repeating it.
Among models whose code is functional, probed interference is again strictly
monotone in $k$ (seed 1: $.232 > .177 > .065 > .013$) and tracks normalized
leakage at $r{=}0.70$ ($0.90$ on TinyStories). The instructive point is the
excluded one. The second $k{=}16$ seed pairs the fleet-worst normalized
leakage ($2.75$) with near-clean code geometry ($.056$, below $k{=}256$): a
model can abandon its code and keep leaking, and the code-space probe cannot
see it. This is the cross-model form of the within-model dissociation of
\S\ref{sec:superpos}. Where the leak of an abandoned code actually travels
turned out to be answerable exactly, and the answer (\S\ref{sec:depth}) is
that it changes carriers.

The targeting law of \S\ref{sec:law} took two preregistered rounds here and
ended sharper than it began. Round 1 (ranking registered before measurement)
scored Spearman $0.31$ against a registered $0.5$, and the miss was
structured: the top absorbers (teaching $1.04$, tech $0.91$, academia $0.78$)
are occupation-adjacent, so the source--target semantic independence that
TinyStories' attribute set provided does not hold when the steered source is
an occupation axis. Round 2 made that diagnosis testable. We froze a
selection rule (prevalence $\geq 5\%$; $|\log\mathrm{OR}| \leq 0.5$ of
presence between health- and creative-profession bios), checked that it cuts
in both directions (it rejects exactly the round-1 confounded targets, and
also rejects intuitively neutral candidates the data disqualifies: geography
at $-0.61$, temporal at $-0.98$, featured at $-2.26$), and registered
per-(model, target) predictions, per-model natural variation times
movability, for the seven surviving targets across all eight models, 56
cells. The registered pooled test failed (Spearman $0.074$, Pearson
$-0.45$), for one pre-flagged reason: every cell of the second $k{=}16$ seed
was predicted near zero (its natural variation at fixed code reads
${\approx}0$) and every one measured fleet-maximum leakage ($2.3$--$3.9$).
The preregistered companion excluding that one model passed cleanly
($\rho{=}0.599$, $p{<}10^{-4}$, 49 cells). On source-independent targets the
law replicates for every model whose code is functional; the round-1 miss
was a property of the test set, and the one genuine violation has a precise
mechanism, which \S\ref{sec:depth} isolates: that model's leakage does not
travel through the denoiser at all.

\subsection{A minimal model reproduces every regularity}
\label{sec:toy}
The full causal chain, capacity $\to$ overlap $\to$ biased natural variation
$\to$ leakage, is simple enough to demand a toy model, and a small one
suffices (\texttt{toy\_capacity\_leakage.py}; independent binary attributes on
orthogonal directions, a learned linear-encoder bottleneck $k$, an MLP
generator, and a manifold snap whose per-attribute basin sharpness plays the
role of natural variation, the analogue of decoding to tokens). With no
tuning beyond matching the protocol (difference-of-means axes, three-axis
decontamination, fixed-code resampling), the toy reproduces all four
regularities of the study: probe interference rises strictly monotonically as
$k$ shrinks; across controllable models interference tracks mean leakage at
$r{=}0.97$ (the study: $0.90$); below a critical $k$ steering and leakage die
together, the capacity-collapse analogue of $k{=}8$; and within a model,
which attributes absorb the leak is predicted by their natural variation
(mean Spearman $+0.44$, positive in five of six code sizes) and not by
pairwise code-direction overlap (mean $+0.10$, sign-inconsistent). The
mechanism the toy isolates is geometric: steering along a decontaminated but
superposed axis pushes the generator off the data manifold, and the snap back
lands disproportionately on the attributes with the shallowest basins. Leakage
is the bottleneck's pressure released through the generator's softest modes.

The real model contains a direct analogue of the snap, and a time-windowed
steering intervention confirms it carries the effect. Applying the steered
code only during chosen segments of the sampling trajectory (unsteered
conditioning elsewhere, noise paired across runs), the first three quarters of
the integration steps each contribute little (on-target $0.02$--$0.44$ against
a full-trajectory $3.9$ at $k{=}64$), while steering \emph{only the terminal
integration step}, the long consolidation leap onto the final embedding,
reproduces $80\%$ of on-target control and $96\%$ of the leakage; $k{=}256$
and the $\phi$-controlled retrainings behave identically. Control is
terminal-dominant in \emph{every} model (M1's terminal step carries
$3.4$ of its $3.5$ total, with leakage ${\le}0.02$ in every window). Leakage,
however, migrates earlier as the bottleneck tightens: distributed across the
whole trajectory at $k{=}16$, and predominantly \emph{early} at $k{=}8$, where
the first half of the steps writes leakage $0.33$--$0.64$ while contributing
no on-target control at all. Under mild pressure the drift is realized where
the trajectory commits to the manifold, exactly where the toy places it; under
extreme pressure the contaminated conditioning bends the entire path, and the
snap merely finishes what the trajectory has already conceded. The paired-trajectory readout is also a third, text-free measurement of
the main result: at matched on-target movement, every learned bottleneck code
leaks $13$--$17\times$ more per unit of control than the mean-pool reference
(final-state off-target/on-target ratio $0.11$--$0.14$ for $k{=}16$--$512$
against $0.008$ for M1), and at $k{=}8$ the ratio exceeds one: the extreme
bottleneck moves the off-target reading \emph{more than the steered one}, the
trajectory-level face of its capacity collapse.

\subsection{Where the code is read: closing the localization grid}
\label{sec:depth}
The time-windowed analysis says WHEN steering takes effect; a second
instrument asks WHERE in the network the code is read. We prepend the
steered and unsteered codes as two parallel $\phi$ token groups (sharing all
parameters, so checkpoints load unchanged) and give every layer a key mask
selecting which group it may attend to: a contiguous layer window reads the
steered code, all other layers read the base code, constant across the
trajectory. All measured conditions run through one graph with paired noise
(the masking's exactness is verified to $4{\times}10^{-6}$ at the forward
level; TF32 matmuls must be disabled, and the base-$\phi$ readout frame
isolates the denoiser's residual response from the additive $\phi$ shift).

The profile is the same in all thirteen functional models on both corpora
(Table~\ref{tab:depth}): control and leakage enter almost entirely through
layers 0--2 of 12, with every later quartile near zero. Depth is therefore
NOT where control and leakage dissociate; they share an entry point, and the
grid closes into one sentence: the code is read once, early in depth, and
the capacity pressure differentiates along trajectory time (terminal-step
realization under mild pressure, early-time migration under extreme
pressure, \S\ref{sec:toy}), not along network depth. One depth-specific
signature does appear on biographies: single-layer steering is
superadditive for leakage (layer-0-only steering leaks $1.9\times$ the
full-depth steer at $k{=}64$ s1), so later layers partially compensate when
they can also see the steer, another face of the conserved-pressure
behavior of \S\ref{sec:mitig}.

\paragraph{The abandoned code, anatomized.} The instrument also settles what
the second BiB $k{=}16$ seed is. Its denoiser output is \emph{bitwise}
invariant to the conditioning vector: perturbing $\phi$ by ten standard
deviations changes no latent by any amount, on the production sampling path.
Yet its decoded text swings wholesale with the steer, because ELF's decoder
head, which is also $\phi$-conditioned, still reads the code that the
denoiser has severed; the model's generations are meanwhile degenerate
repetitive text, a quality collapse that per-seed gating would have caught.
Every puzzle this model posed dissolves at once: its probe reads clean
(the encoder-side code is intact), its natural variation reads zero (the
denoiser, which natvar interrogates, truly ignores $\phi$), and its
fleet-maximum measured leakage is real but travels entirely through the
decoder-conditioning channel. The targeting law's scope is thereby exact:
natural variation predicts leakage carried by the denoiser's conditional
response; leakage that switches carriers is invisible to it by construction.
For practitioners the lesson is uncomfortable: a conditioning pathway can
die in the component you are monitoring and survive in one you are not, and
every code-space diagnostic will read clean while it does.

\paragraph{Carrier shares, measured factorially.} The anatomy generalizes
into an instrument. Crossing the two conditioning sites at inference,
latents generated with $\phi_{\mathrm{gen}} \in \{\text{base},
\text{steer}\}$ and each decoded with $\phi_{\mathrm{dec}} \in
\{\text{base}, \text{steer}\}$, decomposes every model's measured steering
effect into a denoiser share and a decoder share under the standard
text-level readout (Table~\ref{tab:channels}). In all thirteen functional
models the denoiser carries essentially everything: $0.95$--$1.01$ of
control and $0.93$--$1.06$ of leakage on TinyStories and the functional BiB
fleet, with decoder shares at or below $0.10$ for leakage (BiB decoder
control shares reach $0.28$ at $k{=}16$ s1, a real but minor secondary
channel). The severed model is the exact complement: denoiser share $0.00$
on both quantities, decoder share $1.00$ on both. ``The leak is
manufactured in the denoiser'' is thereby a measured allocation, not an
inference, and the one exception proves the accounting: when the denoiser
stops reading the code, its share goes to zero and the decoder inherits the
entire effect.

\paragraph{Routing $\phi$ at training time.} The decomposition above is
inference-time attribution; the causal version is to train the routing
in. Two additional $k{=}16$ models were trained under the identical
budget with $\phi$ visible to exactly one component: a denoiser-only arm
(decode pass never conditioned) and a decoder-only arm (denoiser never
conditioned), each evaluated with $\phi$ delivered exactly as trained
(Table~\ref{tab:route}). The denoiser-only arm is a normal $k{=}16$ on
every instrument: leakage $0.729$ against the $0.47$--$0.82$ seed band,
natural variation $0.217$ against the $.205$--$.218$ band, carrier
shares $0.93/0.92$ denoiser, and even the same marginal quality-gate
signature ($+.016$ vs.\ $+.008$--$+.018$ across ordinary $k{=}16$
seeds). Removing the decoder's access to $\phi$ removes nothing that
matters: the denoiser pathway alone manufactures the full phenomenology,
leak included. The decoder-only arm is not the mirror image; it is a
failure to condition at all. Steering its code moves no source attribute
($-0.05$ to $+0.02$), its same-$\phi$ generations are less similar than
its cross-$\phi$ ones (margin $-0.10$), and its variation at fixed code
is $3$--$5\times$ the $k{=}16$ band, the signature of an unconditional
generator. A single decode pass cannot even establish control, let alone
clean control. This sharpens the severed-model story of
Table~\ref{tab:channels}: decoder carriage of the kind BiB $k{=}16$ s2
exhibits can arise only as a residue of joint training that later
abandoned the denoiser read; it is not a trainable alternative. There is
no decoder-only escape route from denoiser leakage, because that route
carries no control to escape with.

\paragraph{Depth anchors at $24$ layers.} Doubling model depth under the
identical optimizer budget (ELF-M, $24$ layers vs.\ ELF-B's $12$, two
training seeds per condition) replicates every fixed point and
amplifies the harm (Table~\ref{tab:elfm}). The full-rank floor is
depth-stable on all three instruments in both seeds: leakage
$0.450$/$0.473$ against ELF-B's $0.430$, control saturated, natural
variation $.158$/$.154$ against $.164$. The bottleneck's natural
variation excess is also depth-stable ($.212$/$.219$ against $.210$),
exactly as the capacity mechanism predicts: overlap is a property of
the code, not of the network reading it. What depth changes is the
price. At $24$ layers $k{=}16$'s on-target control collapses from
$0.67$ to $0.33$ in both seeds, while leakage stays high
($0.663$/$1.470$), so normalized leakage rises from $1.2$ to
$2.0$/$4.4$ against a flat $0.45$/$0.47$ floor. Quality gates: both M1
seeds pass cleanly ($+.052$/$+.054$); the $k{=}16$ seeds sit at the
familiar $k{=}16$ margin ($+.020$ and $+.017$ against the $.02$ bar,
one just above, one just below). The control degradation that appeared
only on the harder corpus at $12$ layers (\S\ref{sec:bib}) thus appears
on TinyStories itself at $24$: the ``compounding'' harm is capacity
pressure made visible by scale, not a corpus quirk.

\section{Discussion}
% ===========================================================================
Contrary to the intuition that a compact ``semantic manifold'' code yields cleaner,
more disentangled control, we find a low-rank conditioning code induces attribute
superposition and worse linear separability than full-rank conditioning, while
retaining single-factor controllability down to moderate $k$ on TinyStories
(control survives until the extreme $k{=}8$ bottleneck, where it collapses
together with leakage; on biographies it degrades already by $k{=}64$,
\S\ref{sec:bib}). The
directly measured superposition is the robust core of the result: strictly monotone
in $1/k$ across six code sizes and five seeds, co-varying with leakage at $r{=}0.90$.
The behavioral leakage corroborates this in the mean but is noisier and exposes two
informative departures (capacity collapse at $k{=}8$, parametrization offset at
$k{=}d$).

The interventions and the pair-level analysis then fix the interpretation. The
probe--leakage correlation is a between-model regularity: within a model,
interference does not pick out which pairs leak; the denoiser's natural variation
does (\S\ref{sec:law}), and does so predictively under preregistration. Leakage is
\emph{biased natural variation}: it lands on the attributes the generation pathway
already fluctuates in, which is why it survives every decontamination of the code,
the data, and the injected vector. The probe is a thermometer for aggregate
capacity pressure, not a circuit-level map. The geometry is not the lever: regularizing it reroutes,
destroys, or disconnects, and inference-time surgery finds nothing readable
to remove in any basis we tried (\S\ref{sec:mitig}). The data are not the lever either
(\S\ref{sec:rho}). The generation pathway is: pinning a single counterfactual
direction there delivers the study's only leakage reduction, eliminates the
protected pair's terminal-step realization, and switches off the natural
variation mode that carries it, yet the pressure resurfaces on untrained routes
into the same attribute. In depth, that pathway reads the code exactly once,
in the entry layers, in every model we measured (\S\ref{sec:depth}); the
differentiation between control and leakage is temporal, not architectural.
What remains, and what moves both quantities monotonically, is the dimension
of the bottleneck. The second corpus turns this into a warning with teeth:
where the corpus is harder, the same bottleneck also costs the control it
was meant to provide, leaks more per unit of what remains, and can fail in a
mode that every code-space diagnostic reads as clean: a denoiser that has
severed its code read bitwise, in the fleet's leakiest model, whose leak
rides the $\phi$-conditioned decoder head instead. Conditioning pathways can
change carriers, and instruments aimed at one carrier are blind to the
other; only carrier-level behavioral tests caught it here. Compact semantic
codes do not merely fail to disentangle; they manufacture entanglement,
inside the generation pathway, that neither cleaner data nor geometric
regularization removes, and that pathway-level pinning only redistributes.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The $\phi$ sweep has two model seeds per $k{=}64$ condition
(Table~\ref{tab:rho}), and the $k$ sweep now has two model seeds at $k{=}16$ and
$k{=}64$ (\S\ref{sec:leak}): the aggregate probe metrics and the endpoint gap
replicate across seeds, but Tables~\ref{tab:leakrobust}--\ref{tab:superpos}
report seed-1 numbers and adjacent-$k$ behavioral ordering should be read as a
trend only. The within-model analysis covers twelve lexicon-labeled attributes in one corpus,
with the four core attributes as steering sources; the steerability collapse at
high $\lambda$ has not been distinguished from generation-quality collapse. The
mechanism study is on TinyStories with lexicon labels throughout; the Bias in
Bios replication (\S\ref{sec:bib}) transfers the capacity result and the
localization dissociation but rests on two model seeds per condition and one
source-target pair, its round-2 registered pooled test fails whenever the
degenerate-code model is included (the functional-model result carries the
replication), and the second $k{=}16$ seed is quality-collapsed, which our
seed-1-only sanity gate did not test: generation-quality gates belong on
every training seed. The targeting law's scope is correspondingly bounded:
natural variation predicts leakage carried by the denoiser's conditional
response, and says nothing about leakage that travels through other
conditioned components such as the decoder head (\S\ref{sec:depth}). The
depth-window result is one architecture family at one scale. The training-time generation-pathway mitigation of
\S\ref{sec:mitig} is the directed test the localization calls for; at the
weights evaluated so far it either changes nothing or buys terminal-step
suppression at the price of text-level control, and our inference-time result
rules out only linear surgery on the conditioning vector. Relatedly, most of our readouts use linear classifier directions in the frozen
embedding space; the sparse-dictionary check of \S\ref{sec:mitig} finds the
injected delta at chance in a 4096-feature basis with a validated instrument,
so the vector-content null is not a linear-basis artifact, though richer or
better-trained dictionaries could still read differently. We view the
contribution as reframing this conditioning design from a controllability method
toward a study of how the geometry of the conditioning variable relates to
disentanglement, connecting controllable generation to the superposition phenomenon
of \cite{manifold2026}.
